01 — THE DISASTER

A leak in the night.

During the night of 2–3 December 1984, toxic gas escaped from the Union Carbide India Limited pesticide plant in Bhopal, India. Methyl isocyanate (MIC) was among the chemicals involved. The gas spread into densely populated areas around the plant while many residents were asleep.

Read more: Encyclopaedia Britannica — Bhopal disaster

02 — THE SCALE

500,000+

People exposed to toxic gas.

Published estimates vary according to how exposure, injury, and long-term effects are counted. Amnesty International's reporting on the disaster describes more than half a million people exposed to damaging levels of gas. The figure is presented here as a reported estimate, not a precise count of every person affected.

Read more: Amnesty International — search its Bhopal Gas Tragedy reporting.

03 — THE DEATH TOLL

The numbers are not the whole story.

Official death counts and broader estimates differ, in part because sources use different time periods and definitions. The immediate deaths were followed by years of illness, bereavement, disability, and community struggle. Any death toll should be read alongside its source and the definition it uses.

Research starting points: Encyclopaedia Britannica and Amnesty International.

04 — THE AFTERMATH

The night ended. The consequences did not.

Survivors and their families have continued to seek healthcare, compensation, environmental remediation, and accountability. The disaster remains a warning about industrial safety, corporate responsibility, emergency planning, and the vulnerability of communities living near hazardous facilities.
